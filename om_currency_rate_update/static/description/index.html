Sources

The rates of the source you trust

Choose where the rates come from, and a backup source for the currencies your main one does not quote — or for the days it does not answer.

  • ✓  Frankfurter and the European Central Bank: about thirty major currencies, no key needed.
  • ✓  Open Exchange Rates, with an App ID: every currency.
  • ✓  ExchangeRate-API, on its free feed without key, or with your key.
  • ✓  Central Bank, no key needed: the official rates of 36 central banks, read straight from the bank itself — see below.
  • ✓  The rates are recomputed against the currency of the company, whatever the currency the source quotes in.
  • ✓  Every active currency, or only the ones you name.
  • ✓  Each rate keeps the name of the source it came from; the rates you type in yourself keep it empty.

Central banks

Official rates straight from the central bank

No key, no intermediary: the Central Bank source reads the rates the central bank itself publishes, in the currency of its country — 36 countries today, and the euro area through the European Central Bank. The bank of the country of the company is chosen for you.

  • ✓  Europe and Central Asia: Armenia (Central Bank of Armenia), Belarus (National Bank of the Republic of Belarus), Czech Republic (Czech National Bank), Denmark (Danmarks Nationalbank), Georgia (National Bank of Georgia), Hungary (Magyar Nemzeti Bank), Iceland (Central Bank of Iceland), Kazakhstan (National Bank of Kazakhstan), Norway (Norges Bank), Poland (Narodowy Bank Polski), Romania (National Bank of Romania), Sweden (Sveriges Riksbank), Switzerland (Swiss National Bank), Türkiye (Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye), Ukraine (National Bank of Ukraine), United Kingdom (Bank of England).
  • ✓  Middle East: Bahrain (Central Bank of Bahrain), Israel (Bank of Israel), Jordan (Central Bank of Jordan), Kuwait (Central Bank of Kuwait), Oman (Central Bank of Oman), Saudi Arabia (Saudi Central Bank, SAMA), United Arab Emirates (Central Bank of the UAE).
  • ✓  Africa and South Asia: Bangladesh (Bangladesh Bank), India (Reserve Bank of India, FBIL reference rates), Kenya (Central Bank of Kenya), Nigeria (Central Bank of Nigeria), Sri Lanka (Central Bank of Sri Lanka).
  • ✓  Americas: Argentina (Banco Central de la República Argentina), Brazil (Banco Central do Brasil, PTAX), Canada (Bank of Canada), Colombia (Banco de la República, TRM).
  • ✓  Asia-Pacific: Australia (Reserve Bank of Australia), Malaysia (Bank Negara Malaysia), Philippines (Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas), Singapore (Monetary Authority of Singapore).
  • ✓  The official or reference rate of the bank, the mid rate when a bank publishes only buying and selling rates, saved under the date the bank gives and named after the bank.
  • ✓  Set a backup source for the currencies your bank does not quote: they come from it, the others from the bank.

Schedule

Once a day, a week or a month — per company

  • ✓  A scheduled action updates the companies whose turn has come, each one with its own source and currencies.
  • ✓  A company that fails never stops the others, and the date of its next update is set all the same.
  • ✓  Update Now fetches the rates at once; Test Connection checks the source and shows a few rates without saving anything.
  • ✓  The date of the last update and the result of it are kept on the company.
  • ✓  After three failures in a row, the accounting managers of the company are notified.
Safeguards

A wrong rate never slips into the books

  • ✓  A safety net: a rate moving more than 20% (or the limit you set) from the latest rate of the currency is held back, and the result of the update lists it with the old and new values and its source, for you to check and enter by hand.
  • ✓  The rates are dated as the source dates them, or on the day of the update, as you prefer.
  • ✓  The keys of the paying sources are readable only by the administrators, and never appear in an error message.
  • ✓  A rate of the day already there is updated rather than doubled.
