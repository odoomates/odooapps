At a glance

From the books of each company to the figures of the group

An Indian company in rupees, a UAE company in dirhams, a holding in dollars, each with its own chart of accounts: one consolidation per period, computed, checked and approved.

Group Chart
Group accounts with their type, translation rate and elimination. A default group chart, mapped by account type, in one click.
Account Mapping
By code range, by account type or one by one, per company; the accounts with a balance left unmapped are listed.
Currency Translation
Balance sheet at the closing rate, profit and loss at the average rate, equity at historical rates; the difference to the translation reserve.
Eliminations
Inter-company receivables and payables, sales and purchases, dividends; manual adjustments for the rest.
Differences Shown
What A says B owes against what B says it owes A, pair by pair, before you approve.
Approved and Locked
Month, quarter or fiscal year: Draft, Computed, Approved. An approved period keeps its figures, rates and adjustments.

Questions

Good to know

Does it post entries in the books of the companies?
No. The consolidation reads the posted journal items and keeps its own figures per period; the books of the companies stay as they are.
Who can see the consolidated figures?
Accounting managers and auditors of the company managing the group, with access to every company of the group. Anyone else is refused, with the companies missing named.
What about minority interests?
The ownership of each company is kept, and the companies are consolidated in full. Minority interests are not computed yet.
How are inter-company items found?
Through the partners standing for the companies of the group, from Inter-Company Invoices and Bills, which also creates the vendor bill from the invoice of the other company.
