Part 1

Before you start

1.1 What this app installs

Installing Odoo 16 Accounting Community turns the Invoicing application of Odoo 16 Community Edition into a full accounting application. It installs seventeen modules and arranges their menus, groups and dashboards so that they work as one:

  • Financial reports (Balance Sheet, Profit and Loss, Cash Flow Statement, the annual report, ledgers, aged balances, tax report)
  • Day Book, Cash Book and Bank Book
  • Asset management with depreciation boards
  • Budgets, with actual and theoretical amounts
  • Bank reconciliation, the reconciliation statement and Match Journal Items
  • Bank statement import (CAMT, MT940, OFX, QIF, CSV, Excel)
  • Customer follow-ups and statements
  • Cash forecast
  • Cheque printing
  • Automatic currency rates and currency revaluation
  • Fiscal years, period closing and lock dates
  • Recurring payments
  • Eight spreadsheet dashboards

1.2 Who can do what

Accounting in Odoo is driven by two groups. Set them on each user under Settings › Users & Companies › Users, in the Accounting section.

GroupWhat it allows
Accountantgroup_account_user Daily work: invoices, bills, payments, journal entries, reconciliation, the reports, assets and budgets.
Accounting Managergroup_account_manager Everything above, plus the configuration: journals, chart of accounts, fiscal years, lock dates, period closing, currency revaluation, and the right to post an invoice over the credit limit of the customer.

Tip

Give the bookkeeper Accountant and keep Accounting Manager for whoever owns the period close. Several safeguards in this suite (lock dates, over-budget blocking, revaluation) exist precisely so that the two roles differ.

1.3 Finding your way around

Everything lives in the Accounting application. Its top menus are:

MenuWhat is in it

1.4 Upgrading from an earlier 18 release

This guide describes release 1.3.0 of October 2026. If an earlier release of the suite already runs on your Odoo 16, upgrade it as a whole: this release adds modules and changes several of the others.

  1. Back up the database before anything else.
  2. Replace all the Odoo Mates accounting modules with this release, not only this app. The new ones — reconciliation, statement import, cheque printing, cash forecast, currency rates and revaluation, and the dashboards — must be in your addons path too, or the upgrade stops.
  3. Restart Odoo, then Apps › Update Apps List, in developer mode.
  4. Upgrade all the modules of the suite that are installed, together: Odoo upgrades a module and the ones depending on it, not the modules it depends on, so upgrading Odoo 16 Accounting Community alone is not enough. The new modules install themselves with it. From the command line, -u accounting_pdf_reports,om_account_accountant,om_account_asset,om_account_budget,om_account_daily_reports,om_account_followup,om_fiscal_year,om_recurring_payments (or -u all).

Good to know

Your data is kept: the upgrade only adds to it. Nothing new switches itself on either — the automatic currency rates, the automatic reconciliation and the blocking over the credit limit stay off until you set them up (sections 2.13 and 11).

Part 2

The settings, one screen at a time

2.1 Do this before the first entry

Almost everything on Configuration › Settings can be changed later. A few things cannot, or cost a day of corrections if you change them once entries exist. Work down this part before you post anything, tick what applies to you in section 2.14, and leave the rest alone — a setting you do not need is a feature you do not have to understand.

Watch out

The three that are hard to undo: the fiscal localisation (it cannot be changed once a journal entry is posted), Anglo-Saxon accounting, and the currency of the company. Decide those first.

2.2 The fiscal localisation, and the chart it installs

Configuration › Settings › Fiscal Localization. The package is your country's accounting kit: a chart of accounts, its taxes, the default journals, the fiscal positions and the tax report of that country. Odoo Community ships one for most countries, and installing it is the single biggest saving of the whole setup.

  1. Set the country of the company first, under Settings › Users & Companies › Companies. The list of packages follows it.
  2. Choose the Package and save. Odoo installs the localisation module and loads the accounts, the taxes and the journals.
  3. If no package fits, take the generic chart and build on it. Do not start from an empty chart unless you enjoy typing account types.

Watch out

The package can only be selected while no journal entry has been posted. On a database that has been used, the field is closed — changing the chart then means a new database or a manual chart. This is the one setting to get right on day one.

What to do with the chart once it is installed is section 3.2.

2.3 Taxes: the defaults, and how they are rounded

Configuration › Settings › Taxes.

SettingWhat to do with it
Default Sale Tax
Default Purchase Tax		 The tax put on a new product, and on an invoice line that has no product. The localisation sets these; change them only if your standard rate is not the one it chose. A product with its own taxes ignores them.
Default Sales Price Include Whether the prices you type already include the tax. Retail and most of the Gulf work tax-included; business-to-business usually does not. It decides how every price on a document is read, so set it before you load products.
Rounding Method Round per Tax adds the lines up first and rounds the tax once, on the total. Round per Line rounds the tax of each line and adds the rounded amounts. See below.
Cash Basis Declare the tax when the invoice is paid rather than when it is issued. Turn it on only if your tax office works that way, and give it its own journal and base account — it posts a second set of tax entries.
Rounding of Tax Return, periodicity How the tax report is rounded and how often you file. Set them with your tax return, not before. The periodicity reminders are an Enterprise feature; here the Tax Report offers the period shortcuts instead (section 13.4).

Good to know

The rounding method is not a matter of taste. On a hundred lines of 0.15 at 5 %, rounding per line and rounding per tax differ by a few cents, and the difference lands in the tax account. Most tax administrations tell you which one they expect; if yours does not, keep Odoo's default — Round per Tax — and keep it for good. Changing it later makes old and new documents disagree.

2.4 Rounding the total of a document

This is a different thing from the tax rounding above: it rounds the total of the invoice, for countries whose smallest coin is bigger than a cent.

  1. Turn on Total Rounding in Configuration › Settings › Taxes, and set the profit and loss accounts the rounding difference is posted to.
  2. Open Configuration › Accounting › Cash Roundings and create a method: the Precision (0.05 for a five-cent coin, 100 for a currency with no small change), the Method — Up, Down or Nearest — and the Strategy: add a rounding line to the invoice, or modify the tax amount.
  3. Put the method on the invoices that need it, or on the customer.

Leave the whole thing off if your currency rounds to the cent, which is most of them.

2.5 Anglo-Saxon accounting

Configuration › Settings › Stock Valuation, on a database that holds stock.

With Anglo-Saxon accounting on, the cost of a good is recorded as an expense when it is invoiced to the customer, against a stock interim account, so that the cost sits in the same period as the revenue it earned. With it off — Continental accounting — the cost is recorded when the goods are received.

Which one you use is decided by your accounting tradition, not by preference: the English-speaking world and most international groups use Anglo-Saxon, much of continental Europe does not.

Watch out

Change this only on an empty database. Switching it once stock has moved leaves the interim accounts holding balances that belong to the other method, and they have to be cleared by hand.

2.6 Analytic accounting

Configuration › Settings › Analytics. Analytic accounting is the second dimension of your books: the same expense is posted once to an account for the financial statements, and once to a project, a department, a branch or a vehicle for management.

Turn it on if you need to answer “what did this cost us?” about something that is not a general ledger account. Then create the plans and the analytic accounts under Configuration › Analytic Accounting › Analytic Accounts, and fill the Analytic Distribution column on invoice lines, journal entries, recurring templates, budget lines and reconciliation counterparts.

Good to know

Leave it off and the reports drop the column rather than printing an empty stripe: the analytic column of the General Ledger and of the journal entry print, and the analytic filters of the report wizards, appear only when the feature is on. Budget lines can still be measured by budgetary position without it (section 8.1).

2.7 More than one currency

Configuration › Settings › Currencies.

  1. Check the currency of the company first. It is the currency your books are kept in and every report is printed in, and changing it once entries exist does not re-convert them.
  2. Turn on Multi-Currencies, then activate the currencies you trade in under Configuration › Accounting › Currencies. A currency that is not active cannot be chosen anywhere.
  3. Set the Exchange Gain or Loss Journal and the gain and loss accounts. This is what Odoo posts the difference to when an invoice is paid at a rate other than the one it was issued at, and it will fail at the worst moment if it is empty.

How to work with foreign currency documents is part 11.

2.8 Where the rates come from

A rate typed by hand once a month is a rate that is wrong for twenty-nine days. This suite fetches them for you, from Configuration › Settings › Automatic Currency Rates.

SourceWhat you need
Frankfurter
European Central Bank		 Nothing. About thirty major currencies, published on working days. Start here.
Open Exchange Rates An App ID from their site. Every currency, including the ones the banks do not quote.
ExchangeRate-API A key. A good second source.

Set how often — manually, daily, weekly or monthly — the currencies to update, and a Maximum Change so a source having a bad day is reported instead of saved. Add a backup source for the currencies the main one does not quote. The detail of all of this is section 11.2.

2.9 The revaluation accounts

What you are owed in a foreign currency is worth something different at the end of the period than it was when you invoiced it. Revaluation books that difference, and it needs three settings filled in once, in the Currency Revaluation block:

  • The journal the adjustment is posted in — the miscellaneous one is the usual choice.
  • The unrealized gain and unrealized loss accounts. Unrealized, because the money has not moved yet: keep them apart from the realized exchange accounts of section 2.7.
  • Whether the entry is reversed at the start of the next period. Leave it on unless your accountant asks otherwise.

Running it is section 11.3.

2.10 Cheque printing, and its layout

Two screens, and people miss the first one. Configuration › Settings › Checks holds how a cheque is printed:

SettingWhat it does
Check Layout Where the cheque sits on the page: top, middle or bottom of an A4 or Letter sheet. Match it to the stationery your bank gives you.
Top, Left and Right Margin The nudge in millimetres that makes the print land inside the boxes. Print one on plain paper and hold it against a real cheque.
Print Date Label Off when the word Date is already printed on your cheques.
Multi-Pages Check Stub On when a payment settles more invoices than fit on one stub.

The second screen is Configuration › Invoicing › Cheque Formats, where this suite lets you position every printed field to the millimetre and watch a live preview — the Gulf, UK and Irish, Australian and New Zealand leaves are there, and you can build your own. Set the finished format on the bank journal, under Cheque Format, and choose Configurable Cheque Format as the Check Layout of the company. That is section 5.7.

2.11 The Gulf states

Set the company's country to Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia or the United Arab Emirates and pick that country's fiscal localisation (section 2.2). You get its chart of accounts, its VAT rates — 5 % in most of the six, 15 % in Saudi Arabia — and its tax report.

One setting appears only for those six, in Configuration › Settings › Invoicing:

  • Gulf Cooperation Council Format — prints invoices, credit notes, debit notes, vendor bills and refunds in Arabic and English, side by side, which is what the tax authorities of the region require.

Two more things worth setting in the region: the company's VAT number on the company record, so it prints on every document, and a cheque format from the Gulf leaves (section 2.10) — cheques are still ordinary practice there.

Good to know

The Arabic invoice needs the Arabic language installed: Settings › Translations › Languages. Install it before you turn the dual language format on, or the second column prints in English twice.

2.12 Fiscal years, and the dates you lock

Turn on Fiscal Years in the settings if your year does not run from January to December, or if you want to close month by month. Then set the last day of the financial year, and create the years and their periods as in section 3.5.

The lock dates live in the same area and are the strongest control in the suite; they are worth a section of their own, 12.1. Do not set them now — set them the first time you close a period.

Part 3

Setting the books up

3.1 The order to do it in

Work through this once, in this order. Each step depends on the one before it.

  1. The settings — part 2, and the fiscal localisation first of all.
  2. Chart of accounts — the accounts you will post to.
  3. Journals — sales, purchase, bank, cash, miscellaneous.
  4. Outstanding accounts — on every payment method of every bank and cash journal.
  5. Taxes — the rates you charge and pay.
  6. Fiscal years and periods — the calendar your books close on.
  7. Opening balances — what you owned and owed on day one.

3.2 Chart of accounts

Configuration › Accounting › Chart of Accounts.

Odoo installs a chart for your country when you pick the fiscal localisation in the settings (section 2.2). Do that first, then adjust it — it is far less work than building a chart by hand.

  1. Go through the accounts and set the Type correctly. The type, not the code, is what drives the reports: a Balance Sheet is built from the receivable, payable, bank, fixed asset, current asset, current liability and equity types, and a Profit and Loss from the income and expense types.
  2. Mark the accounts that must be reconciled — receivable, payable, and any suspense or transit account — with Allow Reconciliation. Without it, those accounts will not appear in Match Journal Items.
  3. Deprecate rather than delete the accounts you will not use: an account with entries cannot be deleted.
  4. Keep the codes unique, and keep them in the ranges your localisation uses. The code is what an auditor reads first.

Watch out

Account types matter more than you think. Every report in this suite — and every dashboard — is computed from account types so that it works with any chart of accounts. An expense account typed as a current asset will quietly disappear from the Profit and Loss.

3.3 Journals

Configuration › Accounting › Journals. A journal groups entries of one kind and gives them their numbering.

TypeUse it forSet on it
SalesCustomer invoices and credit notes.Default income account, sequence prefix.
PurchaseVendor bills and refunds.Default expense account, sequence prefix.
BankOne per bank account.Account number, default account, suspense account, cheque format, statement file map.
CashThe till, petty cash.Default account, suspense account.
MiscellaneousManual entries, payroll, depreciation, revaluation, opening balance.Nothing in particular; keep one general journal for adjustments.
  1. Create one bank journal per real bank account and write the account number on it. The statement import uses that number to find the right journal by itself.
  2. Check the suspense account of each bank and cash journal. Reconciliation puts whatever is not yet matched there, so it should be an account you can see and empty.
  3. Give each journal a short, distinctive code: it is what appears in the Day Book and the ledgers.

3.4 Outstanding accounts, and the In Payment state

Every bank and cash journal carries two tabs, Incoming Payments and Outgoing Payments. Each holds a payment method with an Outstanding Account, and that one setting decides whether an invoice becomes Paid when you register the payment, or waits in In Payment until the bank confirms it.

How you workSet the outstanding account toWhat happens
You import and reconcile bank statements A separate Outstanding Receipts and Outstanding Payments account. Registering a payment puts the invoice In Payment; reconciling the bank transaction with it moves the invoice to Paid. The bank account in Odoo only ever shows what the bank has confirmed.
You do not reconcile statements The account of the journal itself — the bank account on the bank journal, the cash account on the cash journal. The payment lands directly on the bank or cash account and the invoice goes straight to Paid. Nothing waits in an intermediate account, and there is no In Payment step.
  1. Open Configuration › Accounting › Journals and the bank journal.
  2. On the Incoming Payments tab, set the Outstanding Account of each payment method.
  3. Do the same on the Outgoing Payments tab.
  4. Repeat on the cash journal, using the cash account.

Watch out

Never leave the outstanding account empty. A payment registered on a journal whose payment method has no outstanding account is recorded and settles the invoice, but writes no journal entry at all: nothing reaches the books, and nothing appears in the ledgers or the open items. Whichever of the two setups you choose, the account must be filled in.

Tip

The first setup is the safer one, and it is why the In Payment state exists: it separates the money you have recorded from the money the bank has confirmed. Take the second only when you genuinely do not reconcile — a cash till that is counted rather than reconciled is the usual case. The Bank Reconciliation Statement (section 13.5) is the report that explains the gap between the two.

3.5 Fiscal years and periods

Configuration › Accounting › Fiscal Years.

  1. Create the fiscal year with its start and end date. Most companies use the calendar year; if yours ends in March or June, set it here and every report that offers “this financial year” follows it.
  2. Generate the periods — usually monthly. Periods are what you close one at a time during the year.
  3. Leave the year open until everything is posted and reconciled; closing it is section 12.3.

Part 4

Opening balances

4.1 What you need before you start

Opening balances are how the books you kept before Odoo become the books you keep in Odoo. Get them from your previous system or your accountant, dated the day before your first day in Odoo.

  • A trial balance at that date: every account with its debit or credit balance.
  • The list of unpaid customer invoices, one line per invoice.
  • The list of unpaid vendor bills, one line per bill.
  • The bank and cash balances, and the statement number you stopped at.
  • The assets still being depreciated, with their purchase value and depreciation already posted.

4.2 Posting the opening entry

  1. Create a dedicated Opening Balance account of type Equity, or use the one your chart provides.
  2. Go to Accounting › Transactions › Journal Entries, create an entry in the Miscellaneous journal dated the day before you go live.
  3. Enter one line per account from your trial balance, debit or credit as it stands. Do not enter a single lump line for receivables and payables — see the next step.
  4. For the receivable and payable accounts, enter one line per open invoice or bill, each with its partner and, in the Due Date column, the real due date. This is what makes the aged balances, the follow-ups and the cash forecast correct from day one.
  5. Check that the entry balances, then post it.

Tip

Enter the open invoices as separate lines with their due dates even though it is more work. A single lump receivable line gives you a correct balance sheet and a useless aged balance, follow-up and forecast.

4.3 Opening the bank

Set the opening balance of each bank journal by entering the closing balance of your last paper statement as the starting balance of your first statement in Odoo. The bank line of the opening entry above and that starting balance must agree.

4.4 Opening the assets

Assets bought before you went live are entered in the Assets screen with their original purchase value and the depreciation already posted, so that only what is left depreciates in Odoo. See section 7.4.

Part 5

The daily work

5.1 Customer invoices

Customers › Invoices. The cycle is always the same: draft, confirm, get paid, reconcile.

  1. New, pick the customer; the payment terms and the receivable account come from their record.
  2. Add the lines. The income account comes from the product, or from the journal if the line has no product.
  3. Check the taxes and the Due Date, then Confirm. The invoice takes its number from the sales journal and the entry is posted.
  4. Send it by email or print it.
  5. When the money arrives, Register Payment (section 5.4) or match it on the bank reconciliation screen (section 6.3).

5.2 Vendor bills

Vendors › Bills. Same cycle, and two things worth watching:

  • Put the vendor's own document number in Bill Reference. Odoo warns you if the same reference comes in twice from the same vendor, which catches most duplicate payments.

5.3 Journal entries by hand

Accounting › Transactions › Journal Entries. For payroll, depreciation entered manually, accruals, corrections and anything that is not an invoice, a bill or a payment.

  1. New, choose the journal and the date. The date decides the period, so check it against your lock dates.
  2. Enter the lines with their accounts, partners where relevant, and analytic distribution if you use analytics (section 2.6).
  3. The entry must balance to the cent before it can be posted.
  4. Post it. A posted entry can no longer be edited; correct it with a reversal instead (Reverse Entry), which keeps the audit trail intact.

5.4 Payments

There are three ways to record money moving, and they end up in the same place.

WayWhen to use it
Register Payment on an invoice or billYou know which document is being paid. The payment is created and matched with the invoice at once.
Customers / Vendors › PaymentsA payment on account, a deposit, or several invoices paid at once.
Bank reconciliationYou are working from the bank statement rather than from the document. Section 6.3.

Good to know

A payment sits on the outstanding receipts or outstanding payments account until it is reconciled with the bank statement. That is deliberate: it is money you have recorded but the bank has not confirmed. The cash forecast counts it separately for the same reason, and the Bank Reconciliation Statement (section 13.5) is the report that sets the two side by side.

5.5 Post-dated cheques

A cheque dated next month is not money in the bank yet. It should not sit in the bank account, and the invoice it settles should not stay open either. A journal of its own solves both.

Setting it up, once

  1. Create two accounts with Allow Reconciliation ticked: Post-dated cheques received (a current asset) and Post-dated cheques issued (a current liability).
  2. Create two journals of type Bank: PDC Received and PDC Issued, each with the matching account as its own account.
  3. On both journals, set the outstanding account of the payment methods to that same account, so nothing lands in an intermediate account on the way (section 3.4).

A customer pays you with a post-dated cheque

  1. Register Payment on the invoice with the journal PDC Received, dated the day you received it.
  2. Write the cheque number and its due date in the memo — that is what makes the account readable later.
  3. The invoice is settled and the amount sits in Post-dated cheques received, not in the bank.
  4. On the due date, when the bank credits the cheque, record the receipt in the real bank journal, then settle the two against each other with an entry: debit the bank, credit Post-dated cheques received. If you import statements, match the incoming transaction against the open cheque line on the reconciliation screen instead (section 6.3) — same result, less typing.

You pay a vendor with a post-dated cheque: the mirror image. Register the payment with PDC Issued, print the cheque itself with the cheque formats (section 5.7), and when it is presented, debit Post-dated cheques issued and credit the bank.

Tip

The balance of each PDC account is then exactly the cheques in hand that have not cleared, and the General Ledger on that account is your cheque register. A cheque that bounces is handled by reversing the settlement entry, which puts the amount back on the PDC account — or by reopening the invoice if the customer has to pay again.

Good to know

One journal per direction is usually enough. Create one PDC journal per bank only if you need the cheques of each bank kept apart.

5.6 Recurring payments

Accounting › Recurring Payments, templates under Configuration › Management › Recurring Templates. For rent, leases, subscriptions and loan instalments — anything with the same amount on the same day each period.

  1. Create a template with the lines of the entry: accounts, partner, amounts, analytic distribution.
  2. Create the recurring payment from it: the journal, the start date, how often it repeats and until when.
  3. Odoo generates the entries on schedule. With Auto-Post Entries they are posted as well; without it they wait in draft for you to check.

5.7 Printing cheques

Configuration › Invoicing › Cheque Formats, then print from the vendor payment. The page layout and the margins live in the settings (section 2.10).

  1. Pick the shipped format closest to your cheque — cheque on top, in the middle or at the bottom of an A4 or Letter page, or one of the cheque leaves (US, Canada, UK and Ireland, the Gulf, Australia and New Zealand).
  2. Duplicate it and set the paper size and the position of every printed field in millimetres. The preview on the form shows a test print with sample values and follows every change.
  3. Print the test page on plain paper, hold it against a real cheque, and correct with Move Right and Move Down until it lines up.
  4. Set the finished format on the bank journal, under Cheque Format, and choose Configurable Cheque Format as the Check Layout in Settings › Checks.
  5. Pay a vendor with the Checks payment method, then Print Checks.

Tip

Leave Print the MICR Line off when you print on the pre-printed cheques of your bank — the magnetic line is already on the paper. Turn it on only for blank cheque stock, and upload an E-13B font.

Part 6

Bank and cash

6.1 Importing the statement

The Import Statement action on the bank card of the Accounting › Dashboard.

  1. Drop in the files your bank gives you. Each file is handed to the parsers in turn and read by the first one that recognises it, so you never have to say which format it is: CAMT.053, MT940, OFX/QFX, QIF, or CSV and Excel.
  2. Leave the Journal empty if the file carries the bank account number: the right journal is found from it. A file imported into the journal of another account is refused.
  3. For CSV and Excel, choose a File Map. Once it is set on the journal it is filled in automatically at every later import. Without one, the map Date, Label, Reference, Partner, Amount, Currency reads the files of the earlier versions of this module for 16.0, as they were.
  4. Import. Transactions already imported are skipped — the identifier the bank gives each transaction is kept — and the message tells you how many were new and how many were already there.

6.2 Building a file map for your bank

Configuration › Bank Statement File Maps. A map describes one bank's CSV or Excel export, once.

SettingWhat to put in it
Encoding, Delimiter, Text Delimiterutf-8 and a comma for most banks; write tab for a TSV.
Decimal / Thousands SeparatorMatch how your bank writes 1,234.56 or 1.234,56 or 1 234,56.
Date FormatPython notation: %d/%m/%Y, %m/%d/%y, %Y-%m-%d.
Rows to Skip, Has a Header Row, SheetSkip the preamble many banks put above the table.
Amount, or Debit and CreditOne signed column, or two. Debit is Positive if your bank does not use a minus sign.
Label, Reference, Partner, Account Number, Currency, Notes, Unique IDName the columns by their header, or by their number when the file has none. The Unique ID column is what stops a re-import duplicating transactions.

6.3 Reconciling the bank

Accounting › Reconciliation › Bank Reconciliation, or the Reconcile button on the bank card of the dashboard.

  1. Pick the journal and filter on To Reconcile. For the selected transaction, Odoo lists the open journal items most likely to be its counterpart, ranked by amount, reference, partner and direction.
  2. If a suggestion is right, select it and Validate. You can match one item, several, or only part of an amount.
  3. If nothing fits, search the open items yourself, or add a counterpart by hand with its account, label, partner, taxes and analytic distribution — bank charges and interest are done this way.
  4. Apply a reconciliation model to fill the counterparts in from its lines for anything you do repeatedly.
  5. Whatever is not covered stays on the suspense account, so nothing is ever lost.
  6. Mark a transaction to check when you are unsure; it stays visible for a colleague.

Tip

Let the automatic reconciliation do the repetitive part: reconciliation models with an automatic trigger, partner mapping models that fill in the partner of a new transaction, and the rule that reconciles a transaction matching exactly one open item by amount and reference. A scheduled action treats new transactions daily and runs again as soon as more are imported.

6.4 Match Journal Items

Accounting › Reconciliation › Match Journal Items. For everything that is not a bank transaction: an invoice paid by a credit note, an advance applied to a bill, two entries that cancel out.

  1. The open items of the reconcilable accounts are listed, grouped by account and partner.
  2. Select the items that belong together and press Match. The wizard shows the debit, the credit and the difference.
  3. Write the difference off on a journal, account, date and label of your choice — or leave it, and the items are matched partially.
  4. Match Suggestions reconciles, in one go, every group of items of the same account and partner whose amounts cancel each other.

6.5 Cash

A cash journal works the same way with two differences: New Transaction types the movements in by hand, and New Statement records the cash counted at the end of the day. Reconcile the counted amount against the movements so the till agrees with the books, then print the Cash Book (section 13.6).

Part 7

Assets

7.1 Asset categories

Configuration › Management › Asset Category. A category is a template: set it up once and every asset of that kind inherits it.

  • Asset account — where the gross value sits.
  • Depreciation account — the accumulated depreciation, on the balance sheet.
  • Expense account — where the periodic charge is posted.
  • Journal — usually the miscellaneous one.
  • Method — linear or degressive, the number of depreciations and their period.
  • Prorata temporis — start depreciating on the purchase date rather than the first of the period.

7.2 Creating an asset

  1. Configuration › Accounting › Assets › New: name, category, purchase value and date. The board is computed as you type.
  2. Or let the vendor bill do it: put the asset category on the bill line and confirm the bill — the asset is created and linked to it.
  3. Check the depreciation board: one line per period with its date, amount and remaining value.
  4. Confirm the asset to put it in service.

7.3 Posting depreciation

Each board line is posted in its period, either by the scheduled action or with Create Move on the line. The entry debits the expense account and credits the accumulated depreciation.

When an asset is sold or scrapped, use Sell or Dispose: the remaining value is depreciated in one last entry and the asset is closed. Invoice the sale itself to the buyer as usual. Modify Depreciation changes the number or the length of the periods still to come, from the next one.

7.4 Assets that already existed

For an asset bought before you went live, create it with the value still to depreciate — the purchase value less the depreciation already booked — and the number of depreciations left, so that only the remaining value depreciates in Odoo. The opening entry (section 4.2) carries the gross value and the accumulated depreciation on the balance sheet; the asset record only drives what happens from now on.

7.5 Asset reports

Reporting › Management › Assets gives the gross value, the depreciation posted and the depreciation still to post, by asset, category and date, in pivot and graph form. The Assets dashboard of the Dashboards app shows the same figures at a glance.

Part 8

Budgets

8.1 Budgetary positions

A budgetary position is a named group of accounts — “Operating expenses”, “Product sales” — and it is what a budget line is measured against. Create them first, under Configuration › Management › Budgetary Positions, and put the relevant accounts in each.

8.2 Creating a budget

  1. Configuration › Accounting › Budgets › New: a name, the responsible, and the period the budget covers.
  2. Add the lines: a budgetary position, an analytic account if you budget that way (section 2.6), and the planned amount.
  3. Enter the expenses as negative amounts and the revenues as positive ones, as Odoo reads them in the books.
  4. Leave the Paid Date empty to spread the line over its period, or set it when the amount is expected in full on one day — this is what makes the theoretical amount meaningful.
  5. Confirm the budget, then Approve it; the figures fill in as the entries are posted.

Each line then shows the planned amount, the theoretical amount expected by today, the actual amount posted, and the achievement as a percentage.

8.3 Following a budget

  • Reporting › Management › Budgets Analysis gives every line in pivot, list and graph, grouped by budget, position or analytic account.
  • The Budgets dashboard of the Dashboards app compares the actual expenses and revenues with what the budgets expected at this date.
  • Open the entries behind a line from the line itself, to see what made its actual amount.

Part 9

Getting paid: follow-ups

9.1 Follow-up plans

Configuration › Invoicing › Follow-up Plans. A plan is a ladder of levels, each triggered a number of days after the due date.

  1. Create the levels: 7 days — a reminder email; 21 days — a firmer letter; 45 days — a phone call activity; 60 days — send to legal.
  2. For each level choose what happens: send an email, print a letter, raise an activity, or simply mark the customer.
  3. Write the message on the level. The customer statement can be attached so the customer sees exactly what is open.

9.2 Working the list

Follow-Ups › Follow-ups. One line per customer with something overdue.

  1. Open a customer to see every open invoice, how late each one is, and the total owed.
  2. Act on the level Odoo proposes, or override it.
  3. Record a promise to pay with a date: the customer drops off the list until that date.
  4. Mark an invoice disputed so it stops driving reminders while it is argued about.
  5. Exclude a customer you never want chased automatically.
  6. Send All Reminders processes the whole list in one go.

9.3 Customer statements

Print or email a statement of account from the customer or from the follow-up screen: every open invoice, what has been paid, and the ageing of the balance. There is a vendor statement too, for the accounts your suppliers send you. Follow-Ups › Follow-Up Analysis shows how the overdue balance is spread across the levels.

Part 10

Cash forecast

10.1 Expected items

Accounting › Cash Forecast Items. Everything you know is coming but that no document holds yet: salaries, rent, loan instalments, taxes, a subsidy on its way.

  • A receipt or a payment, with its amount, currency, date and partner.
  • Once, or every week, month, quarter or year, until a date or without end.
  • A monthly item dated the 31st stays at the end of each month, whatever its length.
  • Archive an item that no longer applies rather than deleting it.

10.2 Running the forecast

Reporting › Management › Cash Forecast.

  1. Choose the start date, by weeks or by months, and how many periods (up to 60).
  2. Decide what to do with invoices and bills that are already due: give them their own Overdue column, or leave them out.
  3. Include the draft invoices and bills for the worst case, and detail the rows by partner if you want to see who owes what.
  4. Print it as a PDF, or export it to Excel with the Accounting Excel Reports module.

The forecast starts from the bank and cash balance of today and adds, period by period, the customer invoices, the recurring receipts and the expected receipts, less the vendor bills, the recurring payments and the expected payments, to a closing balance.

Part 11

Currencies

11.1 Multi-currency basics

Turn on Multi-Currencies in the settings and activate the currencies you trade in (section 2.7). Every foreign currency document keeps its own amount and its value in the currency of the company at the rate of its date.

11.2 Rates that update themselves

Configuration › Settings › Automatic Currency Rates.

  1. Choose how often: manually, daily, weekly or monthly.
  2. Choose the source: Frankfurter or the European Central Bank (about thirty major currencies, no key), Open Exchange Rates (every currency, with an App ID) or ExchangeRate-API (with a key).
  3. Add a backup source for the currencies the main one does not quote, or for the days it does not answer.
  4. Limit it to the currencies you care about, and set a Maximum Change so an absurd rate is reported instead of saved.
  5. Test Connection checks the source and shows a few rates without saving anything; Update Now fetches them at once.

Each rate keeps the name of the source it came from; the ones you type in yourself stay blank. After three failures in a row the accounting managers are notified.

11.3 Revaluing at the closing rate

Accounting › Closing › Currency Revaluation, at the end of each period.

  1. Set the journal and the unrealized gain and loss accounts once, in the settings (section 2.9).
  2. Open the wizard, choose the date — usually the last day of the period — and the currencies, or leave them empty for all of them.
  3. The lines are computed as you type: for each account, partner and currency, what is open in the foreign currency, what it is worth in the books, what it is worth at the rate of that date, and the difference.
  4. Post Adjustment writes one entry for the whole difference, and reverses it at the start of the next period unless you say otherwise.

Receivable and payable accounts are revalued on what is left open on them, invoice by invoice; accounts held in a foreign currency, such as a foreign bank account, on the balance they hold. A date can be revalued only once.

Part 12

Closing the books

12.1 Lock dates

Accounting › Closing › Lock Dates. The simplest and strongest control in the suite.

LockEffect
Journal Entries Lock DateNobody but an accounting manager can post or change anything on or before this date.
All Users Lock DateNobody at all, managers included. Use it once a period is filed.
Tax Return Lock DateNothing that would change a filed tax return can be touched.

Tip

Move the lock date forward every month as soon as the period is reconciled and reported. It costs nothing and it is the difference between a set of books that stays correct and one that quietly changes underneath you.

12.2 Closing a period

Accounting › Closing › Period Closing. The monthly routine:

  1. Post everything: invoices, bills, payments, payroll, depreciation, recurring entries.
  2. Import and reconcile every bank and cash statement to the last day of the period.
  3. Print the Bank Reconciliation Statement and agree it to the bank (section 13.5).
  4. Revalue the foreign currency balances (section 11.3).
  5. Check the suspense accounts are empty and the open items make sense.
  6. Run the Trial Balance and the Balance Sheet and compare them with last period.
  7. Print the tax report, close the tax period and set the tax lock date (section 13.4).
  8. Close the period and move the lock date forward.

12.3 Closing a year

Accounting › Closing › Year-End Closing. After the last period of the year is closed and your accountant's adjustments are posted, the year-end entry carries the result of the year to the retained earnings account and opens the next year. Closing Tasks keeps the checklist of what remains, and the annual report (section 13.3) is the pack to hand over when it is done.

Part 13

The reports

13.1 Where they are

Every report of this suite is under Reporting, grouped the way an accountant thinks:

Each one opens a wizard with the same three questions — which period, which journals or accounts, and posted entries only or all entries — then prints a PDF. With Enhanced Financial Reports on, you can preview them on screen instead of downloading.

13.2 Statement reports

ReportWhat it answers
Balance SheetWhat the company owns and owes at a date, with comparison.
Profit and LossWhat it earned and spent over a period.
Cash Flow StatementWhere the cash came from and went, operating, investing and financing.

13.3 The annual report

Reporting › Statement Reports › Annual Report. One PDF for the whole year end: a cover page, a table of contents, and the statements in the order an accountant expects them. It is the document you hand to an auditor, a bank or a board, instead of three separate printouts.

Choose in the wizard what goes in it:

OptionWhat it adds
Balance Sheet, Profit and Loss, Cash Flow StatementAny or all three, each starting on its own page and paginated so the contents page is right.
Compare with the previous yearA second column holding the same period one year earlier.
Show the varianceTwo more columns: the movement against last year, in amount and in per cent.
Key figuresA page of the headline numbers — revenue, expenses, result, assets, liabilities, equity and cash — before the statements.
NotesYour own text, printed at the end: the accounting policies, the events after the year end, whatever the reader needs.
Approval pageA closing page with space for the signatures of whoever approves the accounts.

The figures come from the same engines as the individual reports, so the annual report and the Balance Sheet can never disagree.

13.4 Partner and audit reports

ReportWhat it answers
Partner LedgerEvery movement on one customer or vendor, with the running balance.
Aged Partner Balance, Aged Receivable, Aged PayableHow old the open balances are, in buckets.
General LedgerEvery entry of every account for the period.
Trial BalanceOpening, movements and closing per account — the first thing to run when something does not add up.

Reporting › Taxes & Fiscal › Tax Report. Pick the period — this or last month, quarter or fiscal year — or Custom and type the dates. The report prints the base and the tax of each tax, sales and purchases apart. A cash basis tax counts in the period it is paid, not the period it is invoiced.

Closing the tax period. An accounting manager clicks Close Tax Period on the Tax Report, picks the journal and the tax payable and receivable accounts, and confirms. Odoo drafts an entry on the last day of the period that empties each tax account into the payable account (tax to pay) or the receivable account (tax to recover). Check it, post it, then pay the tax office from that account. The choices are remembered for next time, and a period can be closed only once.

13.5 The bank reconciliation statement

Accounting › Reconciliation › Bank Reconciliation Statement. The report that explains the difference between what your bank says you have and what your books say you have — the one an auditor asks for at every year end.

It prints one section per bank journal, as at a date you choose:

LineWhere it comes from
Balance per bank statementThe last statement on or before the date. With no statements it falls back to the ledger balance and says so.
Add uncleared receiptsThe payments sitting on the outstanding receipts account that the bank has not confirmed yet.
Less uncleared paymentsThe same for money going out: cheques written and not presented.
Add items not yet matchedWhat the bank has reported and nothing has been matched to — the suspense account.
Cash position per booksThe result, and what the books should be showing.

Turn on Detail the Uncleared Items and each total is followed by the schedule behind it: date, reference, partner and amount, item by item. That schedule is the evidence for the figure above it.

Good to know

The wizard offers the bank journals. A cash journal has no third party to reconcile against — cash in hand is counted, not matched to a statement — so its section would only restate the ledger balance back at you. It stays selectable for the setups that do run a cash account against a statement, and the report says clearly when a section is a cash one.

Watch out

If a journal has no outstanding account on its payment methods, the report says so on that journal's section instead of printing a total. Payments made through such a journal never reach the ledger at all (section 3.4), so any figure would be a lie.

13.6 Daily reports

Reporting › Daily Reports. The three books a bookkeeper prints every day.

  • Day Book — every journal item of the period, grouped under the date it was posted on, with a total per day.
  • Cash Book — the movements of the cash accounts with a running balance; the accounts of the cash journals are preselected.
  • Bank Book — the same for the bank accounts, to hold against the statement.

Cash Book and Bank Book can carry an initial balance row, sort by date or by journal and partner, and show all accounts, only those that moved, or only those with a balance.

13.7 Printing one journal entry

Open any journal entry, invoice or payment and use Print › Journals Entries: one page per entry with its journal, date, partner, reference and status, every line with its account, label and analytic distribution, and the debit and credit totals. It is the voucher to file, or to staple to the paperwork behind an entry.

Good to know

The analytic column appears only when analytic accounting is on (section 2.6). With it off the report drops the column rather than printing an empty stripe down every page.

13.8 Dashboards

The Dashboards application carries eight accounting dashboards in its Finance section: Accounting Overview, Profit and Loss, Balance Sheet, Receivables and Payables, Bank and Cash, Assets, Budgets, and Follow-ups and Cash Forecast.

They work over the period you choose, compare it with the same period last year or the one before, and add up the companies picked in the switcher. All of them are computed from account types, so they work with any chart of accounts. The formula OM.ACCOUNT.BALANCE gives you the same figures in any spreadsheet of your own.

Part 14

Routines

14.1 Every day

  • Enter and confirm the customer invoices and the vendor bills of the day.
  • Register the payments you know about.
  • Import the bank statement and reconcile what the suggestions get right.
  • Count the till, record the cash statement, reconcile it.
  • Print the Day Book if your bookkeeping requires a daily record.

14.2 Every month

  • Post payroll, depreciation and any accruals.
  • Check the recurring payments generated what they should have.
  • Reconcile every bank and cash journal to the last day of the month.
  • Print the Bank Reconciliation Statement and agree it to the bank.
  • Empty the suspense accounts.
  • Revalue the foreign currency balances.
  • Run the Trial Balance, the Balance Sheet and the Profit and Loss; compare with last month.
  • Review the aged receivable and send the follow-ups.
  • Check the budgets for lines going over.
  • File the tax return, set the tax lock date.
  • Close the period and move the lock date forward.

14.3 Every year

  • Close all twelve periods.
  • Post your accountant's adjustments.
  • Check the assets analysis against the asset accounts.
  • Confirm the physical inventory and the stock valuation if you hold stock.
  • Run the year-end closing entry and open the new year.
  • Print the annual report for the file and for whoever signs the accounts.
  • Set the All Users Lock Date on the closed year.

Part 15

When something looks wrong

15.1 The usual suspects

SymptomWhere to look
A report is empty or missing accountsThe account type is wrong. Every report here is built from types, not codes.
The balance sheet does not balanceRun the Trial Balance for the same dates. An unbalanced result almost always means an entry posted to a period you are not printing, or an opening entry that was never balanced.
An account will not appear in Match Journal ItemsAllow Reconciliation is off on the account.
A bank transaction cannot be reconciledIt is already reconciled, or another user has it open: a transaction being reconciled is locked so two people cannot match it twice.
The suspense account keeps growingTransactions were validated without a full counterpart. Filter the journal items on the suspense account and finish them.
A statement file is refusedThe format is not one of the six supported, or — for CSV and Excel — the file map does not match the file. Check the date format and the decimal separator first.
Importing the same file brings nothing inCorrect: the transactions were already imported. The message says how many were skipped.
A payment settles the invoice but nothing reaches the ledgerThe payment method of that journal has no outstanding account. Section 3.4.
The bank reconciliation statement will not add upRead the warning on the section. A journal with no outstanding accounts cannot be reconciled until they are set.
A payslip, invoice or entry cannot be postedA lock date covers its date. Only an accounting manager can post before the journal entries lock date, and nobody before the all users lock date.
The rates stopped updatingOpen the settings and read Last Update Result, then press Test Connection. A paying source with an expired key fails silently otherwise.
A revaluation is refusedThat date is already revalued. Reverse or delete the existing adjustment entry first.
The taxes are a cent out against the invoiceThe rounding method. Section 2.3, and it has to be decided once and left alone.
The fiscal localisation cannot be changedAn entry has been posted. Section 2.2 — this one is decided on day one.
